# Queued work acts in the Organization its message names

Work that runs after a request, such as sending a Campaign's batches, goes through the `Queue` port (`packages/core/src/queue.ts`). Every `QueueMessage` names the Organization it acts in, and its handler runs inside `asQueuedFor(message)`, which provides `CurrentOrganization` from that ID. The message is trusted because only the app sends queue messages, from a request that already passed `asMemberOf` for that Organization; nobody outside the app can write to pg-boss's tables or a Cloudflare Queue. So the same repositories, scoped by `CurrentOrganization` (ADR 0007), serve requests and queued work, and there's still no unscoped query path for tenant data.

`CurrentOrganization` now holds only the Organization's ID. The signed-in person's role moved to `CurrentMembership`, which only `asMemberOf` provides, because queued work acts for the Organization, not for a member: a role copied into a message would be stale by the time it runs.

Delivery is at least once. A handler works from state in Postgres, not from the message, so a message delivered again skips work already recorded. A Campaign batch sends at most once to each recipient: before handing an email to the Provider, a delivery claims its recipient, moving them from `queued` to `sending` in one statement, and records their outcome as soon as the Provider answers. A delivery that finds a recipient no longer `queued` leaves them alone, so two deliveries of one batch, one after the other or at the same moment, never both send to someone. A recipient whose claim is older than two minutes and was never recorded belonged to a delivery that stopped, after the Provider may or may not have accepted their email; the next delivery of the batch records them `failed`, saying so, rather than send it again. A delivery that finds a newer claim it didn't make fails with `BatchStillSending`, so the queue delivers the batch again later, once that claim is recorded or has expired. On Node, `enqueue` writes the messages through the app's own `SqlClient`, so they join the database transaction it runs in: a Campaign's messages exist exactly when its move to `sending` and its recipient snapshot commit. A consumer can't pick a message up before the rows it points at are visible, and a failed enqueue rolls the send back, leaving a draft.

Cloudflare Queues can't join a Postgres transaction, so on Workers `enqueue` writes the messages to a `queue_outbox` table instead, in the same transaction, and they reach Cloudflare Queues from there. When the request that queued them ends, while its database connection is still open, the outbox is forwarded: up to 100 messages at a time are deleted and sent to the queue in one transaction, so a group the queue refuses stays in the outbox, and rows another forward holds are skipped. A Cron Trigger forwards every minute too, for anything a request left behind. So on both runtimes a Campaign's messages exist exactly when its send commits; on Workers a queue outage delays its batches instead of losing them. Each delivery from Cloudflare Queues carries one batch, so a batch of 100 emails has a consumer invocation's 15-minute limit to itself.

## Considered Options

- **A separate, unscoped repository for workers:** a second query path for tenant data that ADR 0007 rules out, and every repository method written twice.
- **Enqueue after the transaction commits:** works on every queue, but a crash or a queue outage between the commit and the enqueue leaves a Campaign `sending` with nothing queued to send it.
- **Enqueue inside the transaction through pg-boss's own pool:** pg-boss could deliver the message before the transaction commits, and the consumer would find no recipients yet.
- **On Workers, enqueue to Cloudflare Queues after the transaction commits (#11):** no table, but a queue outage or a Worker stopping between the commit and the send leaves the Campaign `sending` with nothing queued, and nothing until pause and resume (#17) could get it out.

## Consequences

- Every new kind of queue message is a `Schema.TaggedClass` with an `organizationId`, added to the `QueueMessage` union, and handled in `handleQueueMessage` inside `asQueuedFor`.
- On Docker, a batch whose process stops mid-send is retried soon after, not when it expires. A graceful stop gives the batch up to 30 seconds and then hands it back to the queue, and `compose.yaml` gives the app 45 seconds to stop. A crash or a kill stops the batch's heartbeat: pg-boss notices within its 60-second check that the 30-second heartbeat has lapsed, fails the batch, and retries it 30 to 60 seconds later, because pg-boss adds jitter to each retry. So a single restart mid-send holds the Campaign in `sending` for up to about two and a half minutes. Each such stop uses one of the batch's 5 retries, and repeated stops hold it longer, because each retry waits longer than the last. The recipient in flight when it stopped holds a two-minute claim, so a retry that comes sooner sends to the rest and fails with `BatchStillSending`, using another retry, to record them once the claim expires. A test has a worker claim a batch and then go silent, as a killed one does, and shows another worker gets it within 3 minutes.
- On Workers, a message can be handed to Cloudflare Queues twice: when its group was sent just before the delete that took it failed to commit. Delivery was at least once already, so the claim above covers it: the second delivery sends to no one the first did.
- A recipient is never sent a Campaign twice by the app: not when a batch is delivered twice, at the same moment or after the first was cut off, whether by a crash, a kill, Cloudflare's 15-minute limit or a write that failed, such as when the month's delivery events partition is missing. The price is the other way round: a recipient whose email was in flight when its delivery stopped is recorded `failed`, though the Provider may have accepted it. A batch's job expires only after every email in it could have taken its full send timeout, so a slow Provider can't make pg-boss start a second attempt beside the first.
- Each email costs two statements, the claim (which also reads consent) and its outcome, instead of one per batch for the outcomes.
- `asMemberOf`, `asQueuedFor` and the unsubscribe and preferences use cases are the only sources of `CurrentOrganization`: `@repo/core` exports it as a type only (ADR 0007).
